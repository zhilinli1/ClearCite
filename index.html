          Did the student use AI to help them learn? If so, then it is acceptable.

          Did the student use AI to pretend they did something they did not? Then this is not acceptable.

          — Evaluating 13 scenarios of AI in student coursework, IB
          • 01

            AI summarises key points and suggests references.

            Student must understand the points and express them in their own words. Anything copied must be cited.

            Acceptable
          • 02

            AI summarises counter-positions, student explores them further.

            Ideas may be investigated like a textbook; arguments must not be presented as AI generated them.

            Acceptable
          • 03

            AI finds quotations and the student copies them without checking.

            The student must open, read and understand each source before quoting.

            Not acceptable
          • 04

            AI develops the research question.

            Work with your teacher. AI may suggest examples only after the area is agreed.

            Not acceptable
          • 05

            AI writes a model essay the student rewrites.

            Treated like using someone else's essay. A viva may be used to check understanding.

            Context
          • 06

            AI writes a paragraph used as a model.

            Acceptable as a reference; the final argument must be the student's own.

            Context
          • 07

            Draft pasted into AI to be rewritten.

            Generally not acceptable. The original draft should be marked. Teacher discretion outside formal assessment.

            Context
          • 08

            Essay written in one language, AI-translated for submission.

            Also not acceptable in language acquisition. Bilingual dictionaries and spell checkers are fine elsewhere.

            Not acceptable
          • 09

            AI improves grammar and sentence construction, not a wholesale rewrite.

            Generally acceptable when language is not assessed and argument coherence is unchanged; the IB paper treats it as not acceptable for IB assessment. Disclose either way.

            Not acceptableelsewhere: context
          • 10

            AI "marks" the work and gives feedback.

            Breaks the "one set of written feedback" rule. Fine for unassessed practice.

            Not acceptableelsewhere: context
          • 11

            AI writes a reflection used unchanged.

            Reflection must describe the student's own process; the teacher attests to authenticity.

            Not acceptable
          • 12

            Student hides their AI use.

            Not acceptable even if the use itself was allowed. Transparency with the teacher authenticating the work is mandatory.

            Not acceptable
          • 13

            AI generates an essay structure or template.

            Acceptable if acknowledged when the work is submitted.

            Context

          Scenario numbers refer to the IB's own paper. Where the paper says "not acceptable for IB assessment, probably acceptable elsewhere", both readings are shown rather than flattened.

          AP Capstone (Seminar, Research) and AP African American Studies

          • AI is optional, never mandatory.
          • Permitted as an aid for exploring potential topics of inquiry, initial searches for sources, confirming understanding of a complex text, and checking writing for grammar and tone.
          • Students must read primary and secondary sources directly, perform their own analysis and synthesis, and make their own choices about how to communicate.
          • Interim checkpoints with the teacher are required to receive a score. Failure to complete them results in a score of 0 on the associated task, and teachers affirm authenticity.
          • AP Seminar: a team or team member who does not follow the policy on the Team Multimedia Presentation receives a group score of 0 for that component.

          AP Art & Design

          • Generative AI may be used only during research, ideation, practice, experimentation and revision. Using it to create final submitted artwork is prohibited.
          • Generative features embedded in editing software — object creation, generative fill or expand, background replacement — count as generative and are not permitted in final artwork.
          • Non-generative editing (exposure, contrast, colour correction, cropping, minor cleanup) is allowed at all stages, including final artwork.
          • All AI use, generative or not, must be disclosed in the AP Digital Portfolio for both Sustained Investigation and Selected Works.

          AP Computer Science Principles

          • Permitted as a supplementary resource for understanding coding principles, code development and debugging, consistent with peer-collaboration rules.
          • The student must review and understand the code and be able to explain it in detail on the end-of-course exam.

          AP World Languages and Cultures

          • Permitted for exploring topics and locating target-language sources.
          • Students should engage with AI in the target language, read sources in the target language, and demonstrate their own language abilities.
          • "Ethical, responsible, and safe use of AI in K-12 education requires a human-centered approach that prioritizes meaningful learning experiences."
          • "Choices about the use of AI in the classroom are ultimately made by teachers based on the needs of the students."
          • "AI tools should be viewed as complements to human processes."
          • Privacy: personal information is protected under BC's Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (FOIPPA). Do not enter names, student numbers, or information about peers into tools your district has not approved.
          • Respect First Peoples Principles of Learning and Indigenous intellectual property. Do not feed Traditional Cultural Knowledge into AI tools or accept AI inventions about it.
          • Keep drafts, plans and prompt logs; verify claims against credible sources.
